A data report · 80 pairs of jeans · 20 brands · measured in centimetres

Women's jeans pockets are smaller.
The measurements leave no doubt.

Front and back pockets of 40 women's and 40 men's jeans from popular brands were measured to the centimetre — deepest point, shallowest point, widest and narrowest width. The front pockets are where the sexes part ways: 23.13 cm of depth for men on average against 14.33 cm for women, a ratio of 0.619.

01What was measured

Eighty pairs of jeans, twenty brands, one ruler

The sample splits exactly down the middle: 40 women's jeans and 40 men's jeans, drawn from 20 brands on each side. Women's prices run from $9.99 to $249, men's from $39.99 to $209 — the two groups cost about the same on average ($80.32 vs $81.18), so this is a fair fight.

02Where the gap lives

One measurement stands apart from the other seven

Line up all eight measurements and the culprit is obvious. Women's front pockets are 0.619 times as deep as men's and 0.732 times as deep at their shallowest point. Everything else — front pocket width (0.928), back pocket depth (0.945), back pocket width (0.974) — sits within a few per cent of the men's version.

03In area terms

A front pocket, halved; a back pocket, untouched

Multiply deepest point by widest point and the front-pocket difference becomes brutal: 378.4 cm² of room in the average men's front pocket against 218.0 cm² in the average women's — women get 0.576 of the area. The back pockets, drawn at the same scale, are nearly identical: 219.7 cm² vs 201.8 cm², a ratio of 0.919.

04Every single pair

The deepest women's pocket is a below-average men's pocket

Plot all 80 front-pocket depths and the two ranges barely touch. Men's pockets run from 20.0 to 28.0 cm (median 22.5); women's run from 11.5 to 21.5 cm (median 14.0). The single deepest women's pocket — Abercrombie's Simone High Rise Slim at 21.5 cm — exactly matches the men's 25th-percentile depth, and no women's pocket in the sample reaches the men's median.

Set a phone on the ruler and the picture sharpens: a phone 14.5 cm tall drops into 40 of 40 men's front pockets but only 18 of 40 women's — 45%. 05Brand by brand

Twenty out of twenty brands, no exceptions

Averaged within each brand, men's front pockets beat the same brand's women's every single time. The gap runs from 2.50 cm at Abercrombie and 4.00 cm at American Eagle and H&M up to 13.25 cm at Wrangler and 13.40 cm at Levi's — the brand whose women's pockets average just 12.35 cm.

06The usual suspects

It isn't the cut of the jeans

Maybe women's pockets are small because skinny jeans are slim by design? No. Within the women's column the cut barely moves the needle: boot-cut 14.25 cm, skinny 14.27 cm, straight 14.42 cm. Men's skinny jeans average 22.71 cm — deeper than every women's style, and than every women's pocket in the sample.

07Money

It isn't the price tag either

Women's and men's jeans in the sample cost about the same on average ($80.32 vs $81.18), and paying more buys nothing: the correlation between price and front-pocket depth is −0.151 for women's jeans and −0.262 for men's — slightly negative in both cases.

08The exception

Back pockets are where the sexes agree

Depth at the deepest point: 16.01 cm for men, 15.12 cm for women. Width at the widest: 13.70 cm against 13.34 cm. By area, 219.7 cm² against 201.8 cm² — a ratio of 0.919, against 0.576 in front. Whatever shrinks the front pocket, it stops at the back.

One detail does separate the back pockets: cutout styling, where part of the pocket is cut away. It appears on 9 of the 80 jeans — 6 men's and 3 women's — and those pairs are the pricier ones, averaging $133.10 against $74.11 for the other 71.

About the data

  • 80 pairs of jeans — 40 women's, 40 men's — from 20 popular brands.
  • Pockets measured in centimetres at the deepest and shallowest vertical points and the widest and narrowest horizontal points, front and back.
  • Each record also carries style, fabric, price, and a flag for cutout back pockets.
  • Averages quoted in this report are means over each sex's 40 pairs.

How the numbers were computed

  • Front-pocket depth = maxHeightFront; back-pocket depth = maxHeightBack.
  • Areas = deepest × widest point (maxHeight×maxWidth), a bounding-box estimate.
  • Phone test: a pocket counts as fitting a phone 14.5 cm tall when its depth is at least 14.5 cm.
  • Brand names were normalised for spacing and capitalisation before grouping.
