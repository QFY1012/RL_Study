The pocket gap

Front and back pocket measurements of men’s and women’s jeans from popular brands. Measured with the same tape on both departments, the average women’s front pocket is about 58% the size of the average men’s — and price, cut and brand explain none of it.

01 The gap is real, and it is huge

Every pair in the file was measured the same way: the deepest point of the front pocket, the widest and narrowest points of its opening, and the same four numbers for the back pocket. Plot the front-pocket depth of all 80 pairs and the two departments barely overlap. Women’s pockets run from 11.5 to 21.5 cm with a median of 14 cm; men’s run from 20 to 28 cm with a median of 22.5 cm. The deepest women’s pocket in the whole dataset is shallower than the median men’s pocket, and 39 of the 40 women’s pairs are shallower than the single shallowest men’s pair.

02 It is depth, not width

Women’s front pockets are almost as wide as men’s — 15.18 cm against 16.37 cm, or 0.928 of the men’s width. It is the vertical measurement that collapses: 14.33 cm against 23.13 cm, only 0.619 of the men’s depth. Multiply depth by width and the average men’s front pocket holds 378.4 cm² while the average women’s holds 218.0 cm² — 0.576 of it, a shortfall of 160.4 cm². Width is never what stops a phone going in: the narrowest point of the opening is at least 11.0 cm on all 80 pairs.

03 Back pockets tell a different story

The same tape measure on the back pockets finds two departments that are practically even: men’s average 16.01 × 13.70 cm for 219.7 cm², women’s 15.12 × 13.34 cm for 201.8 cm². That is 0.919 of the men’s area and a depth gap of only 0.89 cm, against the 8.80 cm chasm in front. Whatever constrains women’s front pockets, it is not the amount of fabric the brand is willing to spend on a pocket.

04 Every brand does it

Split the file by brand and the pattern never breaks: in all 19 brands measured in both departments, the men’s front pockets average deeper than the women’s. The most even-handed brand is Abercrombie, whose women’s pairs still lose 2.5 cm of depth (19.75 vs 22.25 cm); on area that is a ratio of 1.10×. The widest gap belongs to Levi’s at 13.4 cm (12.35 vs 25.75 cm), with Gap the worst on area at 2.38× (165.2 vs 393.8 cm²). Wrangler gives men the deepest front pockets of any brand, 27.75 cm on average, while its women’s pairs get 14.5 cm.

05 The cut is not the explanation

Skinny jeans are the usual suspect, but the measurements do not support it. Within the women’s pairs the three cuts — boot-cut, skinny, straight — average 14.2, 14.3 and 14.4 cm of front-pocket depth: a spread of about 0.2 cm. Across the men’s five cuts the spread is 2.4 cm. The tightest men’s cut — skinny, 22.7 cm — is still 8.3 cm deeper than the roomiest women’s cut. Cut moves pocket depth by a centimetre or so; department moves it by 8.8.

06 The price is not the explanation either

The two halves of the sample cost the same: men’s pairs average $81.18 (from $39.99 to $209.00) and women’s $80.32 (from $9.99 to $249.00). And price buys no pocket room: front-pocket area correlates with price at −0.073 across all 80 pairs, −0.177 within the men’s and −0.182 within the women’s — if anything, slightly negative. The priciest pair in the data, True Religion’s $249.00 women’s Slim Straight, has 203.0 cm² of front pocket. The cheapest, H&M’s $9.99 women’s Skinny Ankle, has 275.5 cm². Both are smaller than the smallest men’s front pocket in the data — H&M’s own men’s Skinny, $39.99 at 290.0 cm².

07 What actually fits

Take the phone seriously as a yardstick and the counts are stark. At 15 cm tall — a large modern handset — all 40 men’s front pockets take it and 12 of the 40 women’s do. At 16 cm only 5 women’s pockets still fit it, at 17 cm only 4. Every one of the 80 pockets is wide enough at its narrowest point; depth is the only barrier. The roomiest women’s front pockets in the data are Abercrombie’s two Simone pairs at 333.25 and 333.00 cm², and the third-best is H&M’s $9.99 Skinny Ankle at 275.5 cm² — roomier than plenty of pricier pairs, and still smaller than every men’s pair measured.

08 The whole measurement

All 80 pairs, every number in the file. Front area is depth × widest width, back area likewise. The rivet height — the height of the rivet at the top of the front pocket — averages 7.06 cm on men’s jeans (4.5–9.2) and 6.25 cm on women’s (4.5–7.5), so women’s pockets start marginally higher and stop far sooner. Only 9 of the 80 pairs carry the cutout flag — 6 men’s and 3 women’s, all from True Religion, Guess, J. Crew and Uniqlo.

About the data

  • One row per pair of jeans: 80 pairs, 40 women’s and 40 men’s, from 21 brand labels in 5 styles, priced from $9.99 to $249.00. Two spellings of “7 for All Mankind” appear in the brand column; merged, the 80 pairs come from 20 brands, 19 of them measured in both departments.
  • All measurements are in centimetres, taken with the same method on both departments: maxHeightFront/minHeightFront are the deepest and shallowest points of the front pocket (the opening is angled, so the two differ), maxWidthFront/minWidthFront the widest and narrowest points of the opening, the same four for the back pocket, rivetHeightFront the height of the rivet at the top of the front pocket.
  • Area is computed here as depth × widest width, which is an upper bound on capacity: real pocket bags taper and corners round off.
  • The phone is a yardstick, not a measurement: 15 cm tall × 7.5 cm wide, roughly a large modern handset, tested against pocket depth and the narrowest width of the opening.
  • Two pairs per brand per department were measured, so brand averages rest on two pairs each; treat the brand ranking as indicative, not definitive.

How this was made. Every number on this page is computed from data/measurements.csv (80 rows, no other source). Each finding is paired with the one-line pandas expression that produces it, listed in facts.json alongside this page. Charts are inline SVG generated from the same file; nothing is loaded from the network.
