A/B test report · online medical consultation

Faster answers, flat completion — and one department left behind

Over six weeks, 18,000 users of an online consultation service were split into an experiment group and a control group. The experiment cut the wait for a doctor's first reply by about a fifth and almost eliminated long waits. Overall completion did not budge. In dermatology, however, it fell sharply.

01 — The test

A clean split, run on a growing service

The file holds 18,000 consultations entered between 18 November and 29 December 2024. 8,987 were assigned to the experiment group and 9,013 to the control group — a 49.9 / 50.1 split that is even enough to compare the two directly.

Traffic grew steadily through the period, from 2,409 consultations in the first week to 3,546 in the last, and the two groups kept pace with each other week by week. That matters: if the experiment had been switched on mid-way, or had taken a disproportionate share of the later weeks, any difference in outcomes could be a difference in timing rather than in treatment.

The population is balanced on the dimensions that could otherwise confound the result. The department mix is close — dermatology was 16.7% of experiment traffic against 17.9% of control — and the three platforms (Android, iOS, WeChat mini-program) are within a few points of each other. Roughly two thirds of users are returning patients, again in similar proportion on both sides.

The service covers five departments: paediatrics (儿科), internal medicine (内科), dermatology (皮肤科), gynaecology (妇科) and ENT/ophthalmology (五官科).

02 — Speed

The waiting time is where the experiment clearly worked

How long users waited for a doctor's first reply

Share of answered consultations in each band · labels read experiment / control

Experiment Control

The median wait for a first doctor response fell from 110 seconds in control to 88 seconds in the experiment. The share of users answered within two minutes rose from 53.8% to 68.6%.

The bigger change is in the tail. In control, 385 consultations waited more than five minutes for a doctor; in the experiment only 90 did. The single longest wait in control ran to 71,396 seconds — nearly twenty hours — against 18,669 seconds in the experiment.

This is not a gain produced by one busy department. The median wait fell in all five departments, from 105 to 82 seconds in 五官科, from 107 to 86 in 皮肤科, and by a similar margin everywhere else. Whatever the experiment changed in routing or staffing, it changed it across the whole service.

03 — Completion

Overall, the faster service completed exactly as many consultations

87.94% of experiment consultations reached completion, against 88.02% in control — 7,903 of 8,987 versus 7,933 of 9,013. A difference of eight hundredths of a percentage point is noise.

The same holds for the users who gave up before a doctor ever picked up the case: 5.30% in the experiment against 4.74% in control. Slightly worse, but small enough that on its own it would not justify a decision either way. Seven-day follow-up was likewise unchanged, at 12.15% against 12.31%. Completed consultations did run shorter — a median of 10.4 minutes against 11.2 — consistent with a faster, more tightly triaged flow.

04 — The exception

Dermatology lost four and a half points of completion

Signal, not noise

In 皮肤科 the experiment group completed 80.51% of consultations against 85.04% in control. Abandonment before a doctor rose from 7.66% to 12.71% — 124 of 1,618 users in control, 191 of 1,503 in the experiment. Every other department moved by less than a point and a half, and paediatrics moved the other way, up to 90.37% from 88.94%.

Why this is unlikely to be chance

  • It repeats every week. The experiment trails control in all six weeks of the test, from 83.6% against 84.0% in the first week to 79.4% against 83.7% in the last. A fluke would not hold a direction for six consecutive weeks.
  • It hits every skin complaint. Back rash falls from 86.9% to 79.4%, hives from 85.5% to 78.7%, acne from 86.1% to 82.2%, and the two remaining complaints lose ground as well. No single complaint carries the result.
  • It hits every platform. Abandonment is higher in the experiment on Android (13.3% against 7.2%), on iOS (12.0% against 8.2%) and on the WeChat mini-program (12.7% against 7.5%).
  • It is not a waiting-time story. Dermatology waits improved like everywhere else, from a 107-second median in control to 86 seconds in the experiment. Users left despite being answered sooner, which points at the quality or fit of the reply rather than its speed.

06 — Reading

What the numbers support

The experiment achieved the thing it was built for: users reached a doctor faster, and the painful long waits almost disappeared. It did so without costing completion overall, which is the usual worry when a queue is compressed.

But the aggregate is doing quiet work here. A flat overall number is the sum of four departments that held steady and one that lost four and a half points. If the roll-out had stopped at the headline metric, dermatology would have shipped broken.

  • Keep the faster routing — the wait gains are real, broad and largest in the tail.
  • Hold dermatology out of the roll-out until the cause of the drop is found. The pattern — answered faster, completed less — is consistent with replies that arrive quickly but do not resolve a visual complaint.
  • Re-run dermatology with the same design and a check on reply content, not just timing.
  • Report department-level completion as a guardrail, not only the overall rate.
07 — Method

Notes on the data

Missing values are structural

The 903 blank first-response times are exactly the 903 users who were lost before reaching a doctor; the 2,164 blank consultation lengths are exactly the 2,164 consultations that never completed. Neither gap is a data-entry problem, and neither should be imputed. A further 186 users have no seven-day follow-up record, 36 have no complaint text and 6 have no platform.

Two small housekeeping issues

19 rows carry group labels with stray whitespace, 10 spelled 实验组 and 9 spelled 对照组, so the raw column shows four values rather than two; the analysis strips the padding. Three enter_date values use slashes instead of hyphens and were parsed after normalisation. Neither changes any figure above.

How the comparison was made

Every rate is a simple proportion over all consultations in the group, so it is an intention-to-treat view: users who abandoned are counted as not completed, which is why completion and abandonment move in opposite directions. Waits are reported as medians because a handful of very long waits pull the mean far above anything a typical user experiences.

Caution. These are observational differences between two assigned groups, not a significance-tested experiment. The dermatology gap is large and consistent enough across weeks, complaints and platforms to be treated as real, but the report cannot say which change in the experiment caused it.
