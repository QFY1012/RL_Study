Pockets for him, pockets for her: 80 pairs of jeans, measured

A set of front- and back-pocket measurements taken from jeans sold by 21 popular brands finds a layout that is nearly identical at the back of the garment and strikingly different at the front: men's front pockets run about nine inches deeper than women's.

01 / The gapFront pockets shrink; back pockets don't

Measured four ways — maximum and minimum height of the front pocket, and of the back pocket — the two halves of the garment tell different stories. Across the 40 men's pairs the front pocket's maximum height averages 23.13 in; across the 40 women's pairs it averages 14.33 in.

At the back the gap almost vanishes: 16.01 in for men against 15.12 in for women. The same is true of back-pocket width, 13.70 in versus 13.34 in.

Front pockets also differ in width, but far less dramatically: 16.37 in maximum width for men against 15.18 in for women.

Average pocket dimensions, inches

Front pockets are measured from the top edge; the data records a maximum and a minimum height for each pocket. Men's and women's back pockets are within an inch of each other on every measure.

02 / Every pairTwo separate populations, not one spread

The averages could hide a wide overlap. They don't. Plotted pair by pair, the two groups barely touch: women's front pockets run from 11.5 to 21.5 in (median 14.0), men's from 20.0 to 28.0 in (median 22.5). Only one women's pair reaches 20 in, and only two men's pairs fall below 21 in.

Maximum front-pocket height, one dot per pair

Each dot is a pair of jeans. Dashed lines mark each group's median. Dots are nudged vertically only to stop them overlapping.

A consequence of the pattern: in 29 of the 40 women's pairs the front pocket's maximum height is smaller than the back pocket's. Among men's pairs that never happens — 0 of 40. Men's front pockets average 1.44× the height of their back pockets; women's average 0.95×, i.e. slightly less.

03 / Explanations that failNot the cut, not the price

The obvious suspects are the garment's shape and its price. Neither explains the gap. Among women's jeans, skinny, straight and boot-cut pairs average 14.27, 14.42 and 14.25 in respectively — indistinguishable from one another and all far below the men's mean.

Price against pocket height

Each dot is a pair; the horizontal axis is price on a log scale. Expensive jeans do not buy deeper front pockets.

The correlation between price and front-pocket height across all 80 pairs is −0.08 — effectively none. A pair at $199 sits with 13 in pockets; a pair at $9.99 is no worse off on this measure. Price tracks brand and fabric, not pocket geometry.

04 / Brand by brandThe shortfall is industry-wide

Within women's jeans, the brand averages fan out from 12.00 in at Guess and 12.35 in at Levi's to 19.75 in at Abercrombie. But even the most generous label in this sample stays below the men's average of 23.13 in, and most brands cluster between 13 and 15 in.

05 / The extremesThe four shallowest pockets are all women's

At the shallow end sit Guess's Power Stretch Skinny and J. Crew's 8″ Tooth Pick at 11.5 in, followed by Calvin Klein's Midrise Skinny and Old Navy's Original Boot Cut at 12.0 in. The deepest pockets are men's: Wrangler's Regular Fit (28.0 in) and Slim Fit (27.5 in), then True Religion's Geno Slim (26.2 in).

06 / CaveatsWhat these numbers can and cannot support

The height columns are not always ordered. In 14 of the 80 pairs the recorded maximum front height is smaller than the recorded minimum front height (for example Lucky's Sweet Boot, 15.0 against 16.5). The two columns appear to come from two different measurement passes, so treat a single pair's numbers as approximate; the group averages are what carry the signal.

Small, non-random sample. Two to four pairs per brand, chosen by the original survey, not a random sample of what is on sale. Differences of a few tenths of an inch between brands or styles are noise.

One surveyor's method. Every measurement comes from the same protocol. That is good for comparisons within the data, but absolute values may not match a tape measure at home.

There is also a flag column for a pocket cutout: six men's pairs and three women's pairs carry it. It is too rare, and too unevenly spread, to read as a pattern.
