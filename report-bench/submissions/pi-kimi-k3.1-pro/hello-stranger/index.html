“Small talk” ends with a measurable lift.

The average affect rating reaches 7.38 at the end, compared with 6.10 before the call. Arousal follows the same upward shape, ending at 6.70.

These are averages, not a promise that every conversation improves. The next section shows the variation.

Reading notes

What the report
does—and does not—say.

Two complementary tables

The people table supplies person-level ratings and demographics. The conversation table supplies pair averages. Every conversation has exactly two person rows.

Available-case denominators

End-affect pair averages are present for 1,603 of 1,656 conversations. Person-level change and enjoyment analyses use 3,257 nonmissing post-conversation records.

Gaps recomputed transparently

Within-pair affect gaps are absolute differences between the two raw person rows. Politics gaps are absolute score differences; sex, race and education matches require exact category equality.

Observed response ranges

Affect, arousal and enjoyment span 1–9 in these data. Shared reality spans 1–7. Correlations are Pearson’s r and use pairwise complete observations.

No causal or independence claim

Similarity splits are unadjusted descriptions. Repeated user IDs also mean person-records should not automatically be treated as independent individuals.

Reproducible calculations

Every headline finding is paired with executable pandas code in facts.json. All page assets and charts are contained in this offline HTML file.
